## Terms used in this chapter

| Term | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| **Data clean room** | Two companies put data into a room neither can enter, and take out only aggregates |
| **Confidential EVM** | A blockchain execution environment where contract state and inputs stay hidden even from validators |
| **Agent sandbox** | An agent backend inside a confidential VM, with sealed keys, private memory and verifiable execution |
| **Zero data retention** | A vendor's pledge not to retain prompts or outputs, not to train on them, and not to let staff read them |

## Three things worth stopping on

### 1. The best-funded camp faces the one gap with no budget

Read the table vertically. Camp five — verifiable inference and cryptography — carries the most
concentrated disclosed funding of the six, and the gap it addresses, inference correctness, is the
only line marked "no buyer yet" in the commercial table in
[chapter 02](/agent-trust-stack/en/02-demand/).

Camp six, by contrast, involves almost no new cryptography — RFC 9421 and Ed25519 are both
pre-existing — and yet in twelve months it drew in three payment networks, a CDN and four security
vendors at once.

This does not mean cryptography is useless. It means that **in this market, distribution is currently
worth more than algorithms.** Visa and Mastercard did not need to invent anything; they needed to
announce which signature they would accept.

### 3. Vendor-reported numbers need discounting

Several rows come from vendors' own accounts, such as <Claim id="fortanix-customers" />. This report
tags all such sources as vendor-tier, because **numbers that flatter the source are systematically
optimistic**: "serving more than 100 organisations" may include trials, proofs of concept and free
tiers, which is not the same thing as 100 paying customers.

## A known coverage gap

**This chapter does not cover the Chinese or wider APAC ecosystem at all.** SecretFlow, FATE, the
TEEs on Ascend and Kunpeng, Alibaba's confidential computing instances — none of it has been
researched yet. It is this report's largest geographic blind spot and its main intended
differentiator, since English-language reports generally cannot read that part of the market.

Until it is filled, read this chapter as a map of the Western ecosystem, not a global one.

## How to falsify (chapter summary)

| Conclusion | What would overturn it |
|---|---|
| Distribution is worth more than algorithms | Signed agent traffic still under 1% by mid-2027 |
| Model vendors' confidentiality pledges are unverifiable | Any of them publishes a customer-checkable attestation procedure |
| Vendor-reported customer counts are optimistic | An independent source confirms paying customers at the same order of magnitude |
