"Mature" and "someone is buying" are different things. Plenty of technology is mature with nobody in
a hurry (differential privacy), and plenty is immature with budget already queued behind it (agent
auditing). This chapter separates the two onto two axes.

**Stated plainly: everything in this chapter is judgement, not measurement.** Its value is not that
the conclusions are right, but that **anyone can re-measure it with the same ruler.**

## The rubrics

So the figure can be argued with, both axes are scored against operational criteria rather than
adjectives:

| Score | Horizontal: procurability | Vertical: payment pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Papers and prototypes only | No identifiable buyer |
| **1** | An open-source implementation you can run | Funded by ecosystem, token or research subsidy |
| **2** | A hosted service or public testnet exists | On an enterprise pilot budget |
| **3** | A cloud GA product, or carrying real production load | Compliance-forced, or in a formal procurement catalogue |

Anyone can re-score with these two rulers and overturn the placement of any point below. The scores
live in `data/positioning.yml` in the repository; changing one line redraws the figure.

## What each quadrant says

- **Top right, money moving** — all hardware and governance, not one pure-cryptography entry. This
  corroborates the cost figure in [chapter 03](/agent-trust-stack/en/03-cost/): everything both
  buyable and urgently bought runs at single-digit percent overhead.
- **Bottom left, still papers** — all cryptography. FHE, MPC and zkML sit here, and they happen to be
  where disclosed funding concentrates (see [chapter 05](/agent-trust-stack/en/05-ecosystem/)).
- **Bottom right, buyable but not urgent** — clean rooms, federated learning, content credentials.
  Mature technology waiting for an external force to make it mandatory. C2PA may move up in 2027 as
  the AI Act's content-labelling duty starts to bite.
- **Top left, wanted but unbuyable — essentially empty.** This is the most interesting thing in the
  figure.

On that last point: if this market's bottleneck were **supply** — buyers waiting for something nobody
can build — the top left would be crowded. It is empty, which says the bottleneck is on the **demand**
side. The technology has existed for a while; nobody has turned it into a purchase order. That agrees
with section 2.3 of [chapter 02](/agent-trust-stack/en/02-demand/): across the six procurement lines,
what is missing is not technology but a budget line.

## What this figure must not be used for

- **Not for selection.** Further right means easier to buy, not better suited to your case.
- **Not for valuation.** The vertical axis measures whether anyone is paying now, not what something
  will be worth.
- **Not for comparison across time.** This is a 2026-09 snapshot, and the "cloud GA" criterion drifts
  upward on its own.

## How to falsify (chapter summary)

| Conclusion | What would overturn it |
|---|---|
| The top right is all hardware and governance | A pure-cryptography technology reaches cloud GA and a procurement catalogue |
| The empty top left means demand is the bottleneck | A clearly articulated purchase requirement that nobody can satisfy |
| zkML's payment pressure is below 2 | Naming an enterprise buyer with it in a formal budget |
