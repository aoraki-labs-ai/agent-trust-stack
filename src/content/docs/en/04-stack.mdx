## Terms used in this chapter

| Term | In one sentence |
|---|---|
| **Remote attestation** | The chip signs a report stating which code and configuration it is running |
| **NRAS** | NVIDIA's attestation service, which verifies workload integrity before execution |
| **Confidential VM** | A whole virtual machine inside a TEE, rather than a single process |
| **TEE-I/O** | Extending the TEE's protection onto the buses between devices, not just memory |

## How to read it

**Going up, the number of products grows, unit prices fall, and things get easier to replace.**

- **L0 is the most concentrated.** The vendors capable of GPU confidential computing can be counted
  on one hand. The moat here is the ability to tape out silicon — which also means any flaw at this
  layer (see [chapter 07](/agent-trust-stack/en/07-cold-water/)) propagates to every layer above.
- **L1 holds the only seam.** Remote attestation belongs to confidentiality and verifiability at
  once: it is itself a statement about hardware state that a third party can check. It is the only
  thing in the stack that upgrades "trust me" into "check for yourself".
- **L2 is the hardest place to stand.** Standardised from above by L3, and squeezed from below as
  silicon vendors move up. A cloud can simply make L2 a VM type, leaving independent vendors
  compressed between the two.
- **L3 is a standards war.** Three parallel proposals from the payment networks, the CDNs and the
  Ethereum community; see section 2.4 of [chapter 02](/agent-trust-stack/en/02-demand/).
- **L4 has the most certain budget.** It contains almost no new cryptography — process, logs and
  credential management — yet it is the only layer with a regulatory deadline attached.

Standing at L0–L1 is a technology moat; standing at L3–L4 is a distribution moat. L2 is hardest
because it has neither.

<div class="falsify">
<strong>How to falsify</strong>
If an independent L2 vendor sustains high margins without being absorbed by the clouds — public
accounts showing gross margin steady above 70% and net retention above 120%, say — then "L2 is the
hardest place to stand" does not hold, and it may be the control point instead. Executable check:
follow the L2 companies that are listed or disclose financials.
</div>

## How to falsify (chapter summary)

| Conclusion | What would overturn it |
|---|---|
| L2 is the hardest place to stand | An L2 vendor sustains high margins without being absorbed |
| Attestation is the stack's only seam | Something lets a third party verify confidentiality without hardware attestation |
| Competition intensifies further up | Three or more credible new entrants appear at L0 |
